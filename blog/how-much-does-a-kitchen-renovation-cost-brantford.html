How Much Does a Kitchen Renovation Cost in Brantford?

The honest answer depends on scope — here's what Brantford homeowners are actually paying for kitchens in 2026, broken down by project type.

Adam McQuaig · Mar 18, 2026 · 6 min read

The most common question we get from Brantford homeowners is some version of: "What's this going to cost me?" It's a fair question, and the honest answer is that kitchen renovation costs in Brantford range from about $25,000 for a targeted refresh to $90,000 or more for a full gut-and-rebuild with structural changes. What you actually pay depends almost entirely on scope — and scope is something a lot of contractors are vague about until you're already committed.

This post breaks down what drives kitchen renovation costs in Brantford, what each budget range realistically gets you, and the line items that tend to surprise homeowners most.

The three tiers of kitchen renovation in Brantford

We think about kitchen renovations in three broad categories. These aren't rules — a lot of projects fall between tiers — but they're useful for setting expectations before you ever talk to a contractor.

Tier Typical range (Brantford, 2026) What's included
Refresh $20,000 – $35,000 New cabinets, countertops, backsplash, hardware, paint. No layout changes.
Full renovation $40,000 – $65,000 Everything above plus new flooring, lighting, appliances, minor layout tweaks.
Full remodel $65,000 – $100,000+ Layout changes, wall removal, structural work, full plumbing and electrical update.

These ranges reflect Brantford labour and material costs as of early 2026. They're based on a standard-sized kitchen of roughly 150–200 sq ft. Larger kitchens, premium materials, or custom millwork will push costs up.

What actually drives kitchen renovation costs

Layout changes are the single biggest cost driver in any kitchen renovation. Moving a sink means moving plumbing. Removing a wall might mean adding a beam. Relocating an island requires running new electrical. Every one of those changes adds trade time, materials, and often permits.

If your main goal is updating the look of your kitchen without changing how it functions, staying within the existing footprint will save you significant money. If you want to open up the main floor or reorganize the workflow, budget accordingly — and talk to a contractor before you fall in love with a layout you saw on Instagram.

The other major variable is cabinets. Cabinet pricing in Brantford spans a huge range — from $8,000 for a standard stock cabinet package to $35,000+ for custom millwork. Most of our kitchen projects fall somewhere in the middle, using semi-custom cabinetry that gives homeowners flexibility on sizing and finish without the lead times and cost of fully custom work.

The line items that surprise people

There are a few costs that homeowners consistently don't anticipate when they're budgeting a kitchen renovation in Brantford:

  • Permit fees. If your renovation involves electrical, plumbing or structural changes, you need a permit from the City of Brantford or Brant County. Permit costs typically run $800–$2,500 depending on scope. We handle the application and inspections on your behalf.
  • Temporary kitchen setup. You'll be without a functional kitchen for 4–8 weeks depending on the project. A mini-fridge, microwave and hotplate in another room is the minimum. Budget a few hundred dollars for this.
  • Appliances. Our estimates typically do not include appliances unless you ask us to source them. A full appliance package for a Brantford kitchen renovation — range, fridge, dishwasher, hood fan — runs $4,000–$12,000 depending on brand and spec.
  • Lighting. Pot lights, under-cabinet lighting and a statement pendant over the island are often treated as afterthoughts and then added mid-project at a higher cost. Include them in your initial scope.

What a transparent estimate should look like

Any contractor quoting a kitchen renovation in Brantford should give you a line-item breakdown — not a single number with a vague scope paragraph. You should be able to see what you're paying for cabinets, countertops, labour, permits, and trades separately. Allowances — where a contractor plugs in a placeholder number for items not yet chosen — should be flagged clearly and set at a realistic amount, not artificially low to make the quote look smaller.

If a contractor hands you a one-page quote with a single number and "kitchen renovation" as the scope, ask for the breakdown. You'll learn a lot from their response.
