# HandKind Construction Weekly Blog Agent Instructions

## Purpose of This Document

This document governs the creation of weekly blog content for **HandKind Construction Co.**

The goal is not to publish articles merely to maintain a posting schedule. The goal is to build a useful, authoritative local renovation knowledge base that:

1. Attracts qualified homeowners in Brantford, Paris and Brant County.
2. Answers the questions homeowners ask before contacting a contractor.
3. Supports HandKind’s core service pages.
4. Strengthens internal linking and topical authority.
5. Demonstrates HandKind’s project-management, planning and construction expertise.
6. Improves customer trust, engagement and estimate-request quality.
7. Avoids thin, repetitive or generic contractor content.

Every article must serve a clear strategic purpose.

## Document History

This document merges two previously separate files:

1. **The original Wednesday Workflow** — the mechanical steps for producing and publishing a post (template, `posts.json`, upload, photos). This content now lives in **Section 24: Technical Publishing Workflow**.
2. **This strategic framework** — business context, buyer profiles, content strategy, topic selection, writing standards, and quality control (Sections 1–23).

The old workflow's "Post ideas by keyword" list and its simple 4-week rotating calendar have been retired: every topic on that list has already been published (see Section 4: Current Content Strengths), and both are superseded by the Priority Topic Roadmap (Section 16) and the 12-Week Publishing Sequence (Section 17).

# 2. Core Editorial Objective

The blog must become a **decision library for homeowners planning major renovations**.

Most contractor blogs focus on superficial topics such as trends, paint colours and generic renovation tips. HandKind’s content should focus on the decisions that determine whether a project succeeds.

The knowledge base must help homeowners answer questions such as:

- What type of renovation is right for our family?
- Should we renovate, move, add on or build an ARU?
- Is our property suitable for the project?
- How much should we realistically budget?
- What permits or engineering may be required?
- What decisions need to be made before construction?
- What causes delays and extra costs?
- How disruptive will the project be?
- What should a professional contractor manage?
- How do we compare contractors and proposals?
- What should happen before, during and after construction?
- When should we contact HandKind?

The blog should steadily move visitors from early research toward a qualified estimate request.

# 6. Content Strategy Principles

## 6.1 Write for People First

Every article must provide enough useful information to help a homeowner make a real decision.

Do not write only for search engines.

Do not repeat keywords unnaturally.

Do not pad articles with generic statements.

Do not publish an article unless it offers information that is meaningfully better, more local or more practical than the average contractor article.

## 6.2 Support Commercial Pages

Each article must have one primary page it supports.

The article must not compete with that page for the same primary keyword.

Example:

- Service page: **Kitchen Renovations in Brantford**
- Supporting article: **Kitchen Renovation Timeline in Brantford**
- Supporting article: **Removing a Load-Bearing Wall During a Kitchen Renovation**
- Supporting article: **Temporary Kitchen Planning**
- Supporting article: **Kitchen Cabinet Options**

The service page targets the commercial service query.

The supporting articles target specific informational and comparison queries.

## 6.3 Build Topic Clusters

Articles should be created in connected groups rather than as unrelated weekly posts.

Each cluster should include:

- One core service page
- One cost article
- One timeline article
- One comparison article
- One feasibility or planning article
- One process article
- One or more case studies
- One local article where relevant

## 6.4 Prioritize High-Value Search Intent

Prefer topics from the following search-intent categories:

1. Project feasibility
2. Cost and budget
3. Comparison and decision
4. Timeline and disruption
5. Permits and engineering
6. Contractor process
7. Material and scope decisions
8. Local property conditions
9. Case studies
10. Post-project expectations

Lower priority:

- Trends
- Decorating inspiration
- Seasonal filler
- Broad lifestyle content
- Do-it-yourself instruction
- Product roundups without a strong renovation decision angle

## 6.5 Use Local Relevance Honestly

Use Brantford, Paris or Brant County only where the location materially affects:

- Permits
- Housing age
- Property type
- Rural servicing
- Septic
- Wells
- Setbacks
- Local construction conditions
- Contractor availability
- Typical project scope
- Local case studies

Do not create duplicate articles where only the city name changes.

## 6.6 Be Direct About Risk and Uncertainty

Renovation content should explain:

- Preliminary budgets are not final quotes.
- Hidden conditions can affect cost and schedule.
- Permit requirements vary by scope.
- Engineering must be completed by qualified professionals.
- Material availability changes.
- Existing conditions must be inspected.
- Every property is different.

Avoid false certainty.

# 9. Required Article Structure

Use a structure appropriate to the topic, but most articles should contain the following elements.

## 9.1 Clear Title

The title must clearly state the topic and decision.

Good examples:

- Home Addition vs ARU: Which Is Right for Your Brantford Property?
- Main-Floor Renovation Cost in Brantford
- What a Detailed Renovation Quote Should Include
- Can Your Basement Become a Legal Bedroom?
- Renovating an Older Paris Home Without Losing Its Character

Avoid vague titles such as:

- Transform Your Dream Home
- Renovation Inspiration for Every Family
- Upgrade Your Space
- The Ultimate Guide to Better Living

## 9.2 Direct Opening

The first paragraph should:

- Confirm the reader’s problem.
- Explain why the decision matters.
- State what the article will cover.
- Avoid long introductions.
- Avoid exaggerated claims.

## 9.3 Clear Answer Near the Top

For cost, timeline, permit and feasibility topics, provide a direct preliminary answer early in the article.

Use qualifications where necessary.

Example:

> A main-floor renovation can range widely depending on structural changes, kitchen scope, flooring, electrical, plumbing and finishes. The largest cost differences usually come from removing walls, replacing cabinetry, relocating services and renovating multiple connected rooms at once.

Do not make readers scroll through several paragraphs before receiving useful information.

## 9.4 Decision Factors

Explain the factors that change the answer.

Use practical categories such as:

- Existing conditions
- Project size
- Structural work
- Plumbing and electrical
- Permit requirements
- Material selections
- Access
- Occupancy
- Scheduling
- Hidden conditions

## 9.5 Local Context

Add local information where relevant.

Examples:

- Older housing stock in Brantford and Paris
- Rural servicing in Brant County
- Septic considerations
- Property access
- Permit authority
- Common structural conditions
- Local climate and moisture concerns

## 9.6 HandKind Process

Explain how a professional contractor would handle the issue.

Do not turn the article into an advertisement.

Show the process through specifics:

- Site review
- Measurements
- Scope definition
- Design
- Engineering
- Permit review
- Selections
- Trade coordination
- Scheduling
- Quality control
- Communication
- Handover

## 9.7 Project Example

Where possible, include a brief real-world example from HandKind’s project history.

Use only verified information.

Do not invent:

- Client quotes
- Budgets
- timelines
- problems
- locations
- outcomes
- technical details

## 9.8 Practical Next Steps

Tell the homeowner what to do next.

Examples:

- Gather property records
- Define the household need
- Create a realistic preliminary budget
- Identify the desired start period
- List must-have and optional features
- Book a site consultation
- Have the septic system reviewed
- Request engineering input
- Review permit requirements

## 9.9 Contextual CTA

The CTA must relate directly to the article.

Examples:

- Discuss whether your property is suitable for an addition.
- Request a preliminary renovation consultation.
- Tell HandKind about your current layout and the space your family needs.
- Review your basement before committing to a finished design.
- Start planning your main-floor renovation.

Avoid using the exact same CTA in every article.

# 10. Writing Standards

## Voice

Use a tone that is:

- Professional
- Clear
- Practical
- Direct
- Calm
- Honest
- Locally informed
- Helpful without being overly casual

Avoid:

- Excessive sales language
- Artificial enthusiasm
- Empty reassurance
- Contractor clichés
- Corporate jargon
- Long-winded introductions
- Overuse of rhetorical questions
- Repetitive conclusions
- Obvious AI phrasing

## Perspective

Use “HandKind” when describing the company.

Use “you” when addressing the homeowner.

Avoid repeatedly using “we” in every paragraph.

## Reading Level

Write for an informed homeowner, not an engineer or lawyer.

Explain technical terms in plain language.

Do not remove necessary technical detail merely to make the article shorter.

## Paragraphs

Keep paragraphs reasonably short.

Most paragraphs should contain one main idea.

Use subheadings to make long articles easy to scan.

## Lists

Use lists where they improve clarity.

Do not turn the entire article into disconnected bullet points.

## Tables

Use tables for:

- Cost factors
- Option comparisons
- Timeline stages
- Responsibility breakdowns
- Material comparisons
- Decision criteria

Tables must remain readable on mobile devices.

## Claims

Do not claim:

- Guaranteed permit approval
- Guaranteed timelines
- Guaranteed return on investment
- Exact pricing without inspection
- Universal code requirements
- That HandKind is the best contractor
- That a project will be problem-free
- That every homeowner should renovate

# 11. SEO Requirements

## Primary Keyword Use

Use the primary topic naturally in:

- Page title
- H1
- Opening section
- At least one subheading
- Meta title
- Meta description
- URL slug where appropriate
- Image alt text where appropriate

Do not repeat the phrase unnaturally.

## Secondary Topics

Cover related questions naturally.

For example, an addition feasibility article may include:

- Setbacks
- Zoning
- Lot coverage
- Engineering
- Septic
- Access
- Utility capacity
- Permits
- Budget
- Timeline

## Search Intent Match

The article must answer the type of question implied by the title.

Examples:

- A cost article must include cost guidance and cost factors.
- A comparison article must clearly compare options.
- A timeline article must provide stages and timing variables.
- A feasibility article must provide a practical evaluation framework.
- A case study must describe the problem, process and outcome.

## Meta Title

Aim for a clear, specific title.

Avoid unnecessary branding unless space permits.

Example:

> Main-Floor Renovation Cost in Brantford | HandKind

## Meta Description

State what the reader will learn and include the location only where relevant.

## URL Slug

Keep the slug:

- Short
- Descriptive
- Lowercase
- Hyphenated
- Free of unnecessary dates unless the article is deliberately annual

## Headings

Use one H1.

Use logical H2 and H3 headings.

Do not skip heading levels without reason.

## Images

Every major article should ideally include:

- One strong featured image
- One or more project images
- Before-and-after images where available
- Process images for technical topics
- Diagrams or checklists when useful

Do not use irrelevant stock photography when genuine project photos are available.

## Alt Text

Describe the actual image.

Do not stuff keywords.

# 12. Internal-Linking Rules

Every article should normally include:

1. One primary service-page link
2. One relevant location-page link when justified
3. One related educational article
4. One relevant project page
5. One estimate, contact or consultation link

Use descriptive anchor text.

Good:

- Learn more about HandKind’s basement renovation process.
- Review completed kitchen and main-floor renovations.
- See what is involved in planning a home addition.

Weak:

- Click here
- Learn more
- Read this
- Visit this page

## Link Direction

Supporting articles should link to the primary service page.

Primary service pages should also link back to their strongest supporting articles.

Related articles should link laterally where the connection is useful.

## Avoid Over-Linking

Do not add links merely because a keyword appears.

Each link must help the reader continue their research.

## Prevent Orphan Articles

No published article should exist without:

- At least one incoming link from another relevant page
- At least one outgoing link to a service or conversion page

# 13. Cannibalization Controls

Before creating a new article, determine whether it would compete with an existing page.

## Common Cannibalization Risks

- Multiple articles targeting the same city and service
- Separate cost articles with no meaningful difference
- Service pages and blog posts using the same commercial keyword
- Duplicate permit articles
- Multiple “complete guides” covering the same subject
- Annual updates published as separate pages instead of updating the existing article

## Preferred Solutions

When overlap exists:

1. Expand the stronger existing article.
2. Consolidate similar articles.
3. Redirect outdated pages.
4. Change the new topic to a different search intent.
5. Narrow the new article to a specific scenario.
6. Add a comparison or feasibility angle.
7. Update the existing article rather than creating a duplicate.

## Example

Do not publish:

- Kitchen Renovation Brantford
- Brantford Kitchen Renovations
- Best Kitchen Renovation Contractor Brantford
- Kitchen Remodeling Brantford

These all compete with the kitchen service page.

Instead publish:

- Kitchen Renovation Timeline in Brantford
- What Must Be Selected Before a Kitchen Renovation?
- Removing a Load-Bearing Wall During a Kitchen Renovation
- Temporary Kitchen Planning
- Stock vs Semi-Custom vs Custom Cabinets

## Strategy

- [ ] The article fills a documented content gap.

- [ ] The search intent is clear.
- [ ] The primary supported page is identified.
- [ ] The topic does not duplicate an existing article.
- [ ] The article does not compete with a service page.

## Content

- [ ] The article provides a useful answer near the top.
- [ ] The article explains the important decision factors.
- [ ] Local information is included only where relevant.
- [ ] Technical information is accurate and understandable.
- [ ] Risks and uncertainties are stated honestly.
- [ ] No facts, project details or client quotes were invented.
- [ ] The article includes practical next steps.
- [ ] The article is more useful than a generic contractor blog post.

- [ ] There is one H1.
- [ ] H2 and H3 headings are logical.
- [ ] Paragraphs are readable.
- [ ] Lists and tables are used appropriately.
- [ ] The conclusion does not merely repeat the introduction.

## SEO

- [ ] Primary topic appears naturally.
- [ ] Meta title is written.
- [ ] Meta description is written.
- [ ] URL slug is recommended.
- [ ] Search intent is fully satisfied.
- [ ] Related questions are addressed.
- [ ] Image recommendations are included.
- [ ] Alt text recommendations are included.

## Internal Links

- [ ] Primary service page is linked.
- [ ] Relevant location page is linked where appropriate.
- [ ] Related article is linked.
- [ ] Relevant project page is linked.
- [ ] Estimate or contact page is linked.
- [ ] Anchor text is descriptive.
- [ ] The article will receive at least one incoming internal link.

## Conversion

- [ ] CTA matches the article.
- [ ] CTA is not overly aggressive.
- [ ] The reader understands the next step.
- [ ] The article helps qualify the lead.
- [ ] A downloadable checklist or tool was considered.

## Brand

- [ ] HandKind is presented as organized and professional.
- [ ] The article does not position HandKind as the cheapest option.
- [ ] The article demonstrates project-management value.
- [ ] Claims are realistic.
- [ ] Tone is direct, practical and trustworthy.

# 20. Content Maintenance

The knowledge base must be maintained, not only expanded.

## Review Schedule

Review articles at least annually.

Review sooner when they include:

- Costs
- Permit requirements
- Building-code information
- Municipal processes
- Rebates
- Financing
- Product availability
- Lead times
- Regulations
- Service areas

## Update Rather Than Duplicate

When annual cost information changes, update the existing article when possible.

Do not publish a new near-duplicate article every year unless there is a compelling strategic reason.

## Consolidation

When multiple weak articles cover similar subjects:

1. Select the strongest URL.
2. Merge useful information.
3. Improve the article.
4. Redirect retired URLs.
5. Update internal links.

## Performance Review

Use available analytics to review:

- Search impressions
- Search clicks
- Search queries
- Average engagement time
- Scroll depth
- Internal-link clicks
- Service-page visits
- Estimate-form starts
- Estimate-form completions
- Assisted conversions
- Qualified lead value

Raw traffic is not the main success measure.

The preferred outcome is:

> More qualified homeowners reading HandKind’s content before requesting an estimate.

# 22. Final Editorial Standard

Before approving any article, ask:

1. Does this help a real homeowner make a renovation decision?
2. Does this support a commercially important HandKind page?
3. Does this demonstrate genuine experience or local knowledge?
4. Does this article add something the current site is missing?
5. Is it more useful than the average contractor article?
6. Does it move the reader toward a logical next step?
7. Would HandKind be comfortable giving this advice to a client in person?
8. Can every factual or project-specific claim be defended?
9. Does this strengthen the knowledge base rather than create clutter?
10. Is this worth maintaining for several years?

If the answer to several of these questions is no, do not publish the article.

# 24. Technical Publishing Workflow

Once an article has passed the Article Quality Checklist (Section 18) and has been drafted in the Required Submission Format (Section 19), use this workflow to get it live on handkindconstruction.ca. This is a mechanical process — it does not involve any further editorial judgment.

Total time: roughly 30 minutes per post.

## 24.1 Duplicate the Template (2 min)

Copy `blog/_TEMPLATE.html` and rename it using the recommended URL slug from the article brief:

```
blog/your-post-slug-here.html
```

Slug rules:

- Lowercase
- Hyphenated
- No special characters
- Matches the slug already decided in Section 8 (Article Brief) / Section 19 (Recommended URL Slug)

Good slugs:

- `whole-home-renovation-cost-brantford.html`
- `home-addition-vs-aru-brantford.html`
- `renovate-or-move-brantford-paris.html`

## 24.2 Fill In the Template (20 min)

Open the new file and replace every `CHANGE THIS` placeholder:

| Placeholder | Replace with |
|---|---|
| `POST TITLE HERE` | The article title from Section 19 (also the H1) |
| `POST EXCERPT HERE` | The meta description from Section 19, under 160 characters |
| `POST-SLUG-HERE` | The filename without `.html` |
| `YYYY-MM-DD` | Today's publish date |
| `CATEGORY` | One of: Kitchens / Bathrooms / Basements / Additions & ARUs / General (see 24.6 to add a new one) |
| `LOCATION` | Brantford / Brant County / Paris, Ontario |
| `X min read` | ~1 min per 200 words |

Then paste the finished article (Section 19, part 3) between the `<div class="post-body">` tags, preserving the structure already required in Section 9:

- `<h2>` for main section headings
- `<p>` for paragraphs
- `<ul>` / `<li>` for lists of 3+ items
- `<blockquote>` for the key takeaway
- `<table>` for cost ranges or comparisons
- `<strong>` for important terms within paragraphs

As a baseline length, aim for 500–900 words for a standard supporting article. Pillar or cost/feasibility articles identified in the Priority Topic Roadmap (Section 16) may reasonably run longer — defer to the "Recommended Article Length" field from the Article Brief (Section 8) rather than treating 900 words as a hard ceiling.

## 24.3 Update `posts.json` (5 min)

Open `blog/posts.json` and add a new entry at the **top** of the array:

```json
{
  "slug": "your-post-slug-here",
  "title": "Your Post Title Here",
  "date": "2026-03-25",
  "author": "Adam McQuaig",
  "category": "Kitchens",
  "location": "Brantford",
  "excerpt": "One sentence excerpt under 160 characters — same as your meta description.",
  "image": "",
  "featured": false
},
```

Set `"featured": true` only for the post you want to display prominently on the blog index — usually just the most recent one.

## 24.4 Update `blog/index.html` (5 min)

`posts.json` only drives the "related posts" grid on individual post pages (via `js/blog.js`) — it does **not** drive the blog index listing. `blog/index.html` has a hand-authored `<a class="post-card">` block per post between `<!-- BLOG_CARDS_START -->` and the matching END marker. Copy the featured card's markup, update it for the new post (href, image, `data-cat`, category, location, title, excerpt, date), and paste it directly after `<!-- BLOG_CARDS_START -->`. If the new post is `"featured": true`, add `post-card--featured` to its `<a>` class and remove that class from whichever card previously had it.

## 24.5 Upload (3 min)

Upload three files to the web host:

1. `blog/your-new-post.html`
2. `blog/posts.json` (updated with the new entry)
3. `blog/index.html` (updated with the new post card)

Related posts update automatically on all other post pages (from `posts.json`), and Google indexes the new URL within a day or two once the sitemap has been submitted.

## 24.6 Adding a Photo to a Post

1. Export the image at 1200px wide, save as `.jpg`.
2. Name it the same as the post slug: `your-post-slug-here.jpg`.
3. Upload to `assets/blog/`.
4. In the post HTML, uncomment the `<!-- HERO IMAGE -->` block and update `src` and `alt` (write real alt text per Section 11 — describe the actual image, don't keyword-stuff).
5. In `posts.json`, update `"image": "/assets/blog/your-post-slug-here.jpg"`.
6. Update the same image path in the post's `<a class="post-card">` block in `blog/index.html` (see Section 24.4) — it's a separate `<img>`, not pulled from `posts.json`.

For project photos, crop to 16:9 before uploading — this matches the card layout on the blog index.

## 24.7 Adding a New Category

The five current categories (Kitchens, Bathrooms, Basements, Additions & ARUs, General) are hand-authored as `<button class="filter-btn" data-cat="...">` elements in `blog/index.html` between `<!-- BLOG_FILTERS_START -->` and `<!-- BLOG_FILTERS_END -->` — they are **not** auto-generated from `posts.json`. If a post needs a new category, add a matching filter button there as well as setting it on the post's card and in `posts.json`.

## 24.8 Technical Publishing Checklist

This is separate from — and in addition to — the editorial Article Quality Checklist (Section 18). Confirm before uploading:

- [ ] Title includes "Brantford" or "Brant County" (or both), where locally relevant per Section 6.5
- [ ] Meta description is under 160 characters and includes the primary keyword
- [ ] The `slug` in `posts.json` matches the filename exactly
- [ ] Date is set to the actual publish date (not in the future)
- [ ] At least one `<h2>` includes the primary keyword or a close variant
- [ ] Post ends with a CTA linking to `/pages/estimate.html` (or the CTA target defined in Section 19)
- [ ] Schema `datePublished` and `dateModified` are both set
- [ ] `featured` is only `true` if this should replace the currently featured post
- [ ] `blog/index.html` has a new post card (Section 24.4) — `posts.json` alone will not make the post appear on the blog index
