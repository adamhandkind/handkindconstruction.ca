# HandKind Construction Weekly Blog Agent Instructions

## Purpose of This Document

This document governs the creation of weekly blog content for **HandKind Construction Co.**

The goal is not to publish articles merely to maintain a posting schedule. The goal is to build a useful, authoritative local renovation knowledge base that:

1. Attracts qualified homeowners in Brantford, Paris and Brant County.
2. Answers the questions homeowners ask before contacting a contractor.
3. Supports HandKind’s core service pages.
4. Strengthens internal linking and topical authority.
5. Demonstrates HandKind’s project-management, planning and construction expertise.
6. Improves customer trust, engagement and estimate-request quality.
7. Avoids thin, repetitive or generic contractor content.

Every article must serve a clear strategic purpose.

## Document History

This document merges two previously separate files:

1. **The original Wednesday Workflow** — the mechanical steps for producing and publishing a post (template, `posts.json`, upload, photos). This content now lives in **Section 24: Technical Publishing Workflow**.
2. **This strategic framework** — business context, buyer profiles, content strategy, topic selection, writing standards, and quality control (Sections 1–23).

The old workflow's "Post ideas by keyword" list and its simple 4-week rotating calendar have been retired: every topic on that list has already been published (see Section 4: Current Content Strengths), and both are superseded by the Priority Topic Roadmap (Section 16) and the 12-Week Publishing Sequence (Section 17).

# 2. Core Editorial Objective

The blog must become a **decision library for homeowners planning major renovations**.

Most contractor blogs focus on superficial topics such as trends, paint colours and generic renovation tips. HandKind’s content should focus on the decisions that determine whether a project succeeds.

The knowledge base must help homeowners answer questions such as:

- What type of renovation is right for our family?
- Should we renovate, move, add on or build an ARU?
- Is our property suitable for the project?
- How much should we realistically budget?
- What permits or engineering may be required?
- What decisions need to be made before construction?
- What causes delays and extra costs?
- How disruptive will the project be?
- What should a professional contractor manage?
- How do we compare contractors and proposals?
- What should happen before, during and after construction?
- When should we contact HandKind?

The blog should steadily move visitors from early research toward a qualified estimate request.

# 6. Content Strategy Principles

## 6.1 Write for People First

Every article must provide enough useful information to help a homeowner make a real decision.

Do not write only for search engines.

Do not repeat keywords unnaturally.

Do not pad articles with generic statements.

Do not publish an article unless it offers information that is meaningfully better, more local or more practical than the average contractor article.

## 6.2 Support Commercial Pages

Each article must have one primary page it supports.

The article must not compete with that page for the same primary keyword.

Example:

- Service page: **Kitchen Renovations in Brantford**
- Supporting article: **Kitchen Renovation Timeline in Brantford**
- Supporting article: **Removing a Load-Bearing Wall During a Kitchen Renovation**
- Supporting article: **Temporary Kitchen Planning**
- Supporting article: **Kitchen Cabinet Options**

The service page targets the commercial service query.

The supporting articles target specific informational and comparison queries.

## 6.3 Build Topic Clusters

Articles should be created in connected groups rather than as unrelated weekly posts.

Each cluster should include:

- One core service page
- One cost article
- One timeline article
- One comparison article
- One feasibility or planning article
- One process article
- One or more case studies
- One local article where relevant

## 6.4 Prioritize High-Value Search Intent

Prefer topics from the following search-intent categories:

1. Project feasibility
2. Cost and budget
3. Comparison and decision
4. Timeline and disruption
5. Permits and engineering
6. Contractor process
7. Material and scope decisions
8. Local property conditions
9. Case studies
10. Post-project expectations

Lower priority:

- Trends
- Decorating inspiration
- Seasonal filler
- Broad lifestyle content
- Do-it-yourself instruction
- Product roundups without a strong renovation decision angle

## 6.5 Use Local Relevance Honestly

Use Brantford, Paris or Brant County only where the location materially affects:

- Permits
- Housing age
- Property type
- Rural servicing
- Septic
- Wells
- Setbacks
- Local construction conditions
- Contractor availability
- Typical project scope
- Local case studies

Do not create duplicate articles where only the city name changes.

## 6.6 Be Direct About Risk and Uncertainty

Renovation content should explain:

- Preliminary budgets are not final quotes.
- Hidden conditions can affect cost and schedule.
- Permit requirements vary by scope.
- Engineering must be completed by qualified professionals.
- Material availability changes.
- Existing conditions must be inspected.
- Every property is different.

Avoid false certainty.

# 9. Required Article Structure

Use a structure appropriate to the topic, but most articles should contain the following elements.

## 9.1 Clear Title

The title must clearly state the topic and decision.

Good examples:

- Home Addition vs ARU: Which Is Right for Your Brantford Property?
- Main-Floor Renovation Cost in Brantford
- What a Detailed Renovation Quote Should Include
- Can Your Basement Become a Legal Bedroom?
- Renovating an Older Paris Home Without Losing Its Character

Avoid vague titles such as:

- Transform Your Dream Home
- Renovation Inspiration for Every Family
- Upgrade Your Space
- The Ultimate Guide to Better Living

## 9.2 Direct Opening

The first paragraph should:

- Confirm the reader’s problem.
- Explain why the decision matters.
- State what the article will cover.
- Avoid long introductions.
- Avoid exaggerated claims.

## 9.3 Clear Answer Near the Top

For cost, timeline, permit and feasibility topics, provide a direct preliminary answer early in the article.

Use qualifications where necessary.

Example:

> A main-floor renovation can range widely depending on structural changes, kitchen scope, flooring, electrical, plumbing and finishes. The largest cost differences usually come from removing walls, replacing cabinetry, relocating services and renovating multiple connected rooms at once.

Do not make readers scroll through several paragraphs before receiving useful information.

## 9.4 Decision Factors

Explain the factors that change the answer.

Use practical categories such as:

- Existing conditions
- Project size
- Structural work
- Plumbing and electrical
- Permit requirements
- Material selections
- Access
- Occupancy
- Scheduling
- Hidden conditions

## 9.5 Local Context

Add local information where relevant.

Examples:

- Older housing stock in Brantford and Paris
- Rural servicing in Brant County
- Septic considerations
- Property access
- Permit authority
- Common structural conditions
- Local climate and moisture concerns

## 9.6 HandKind Process

Explain how a professional contractor would handle the issue.

Do not turn the article into an advertisement.

Show the process through specifics:

- Site review
- Measurements
- Scope definition
- Design
- Engineering
- Permit review
- Selections
- Trade coordination
- Scheduling
- Quality control
- Communication
- Handover

## 9.7 Project Example

Where possible, include a brief real-world example from HandKind’s project history.

Use only verified information.

Do not invent:

- Client quotes
- Budgets
- timelines
- problems
- locations
- outcomes
- technical details

## 9.8 Practical Next Steps

Tell the homeowner what to do next.

Examples:

- Gather property records
- Define the household need
- Create a realistic preliminary budget
- Identify the desired start period
- List must-have and optional features
- Book a site consultation
- Have the septic system reviewed
- Request engineering input
- Review permit requirements

## 9.9 Contextual CTA

The CTA must relate directly to the article.

Examples:

- Discuss whether your property is suitable for an addition.
- Request a preliminary renovation consultation.
- Tell HandKind about your current layout and the space your family needs.
- Review your basement before committing to a finished design.
- Start planning your main-floor renovation.

Avoid using the exact same CTA in every article.

# 10. Writing Standards

## Voice

Use a tone that is:

- Professional
- Clear
- Practical
- Direct
- Calm
- Honest
- Locally informed
- Helpful without being overly casual

Avoid:

- Excessive sales language
- Artificial enthusiasm
- Empty reassurance
- Contractor clichés
- Corporate jargon
- Long-winded introductions
- Overuse of rhetorical questions
- Repetitive conclusions
- Obvious AI phrasing

## Perspective

Use “HandKind” when describing the company.

Use “you” when addressing the homeowner.

Avoid repeatedly using “we” in every paragraph.

## Reading Level

Write for an informed homeowner, not an engineer or lawyer.

Explain technical terms in plain language.

Do not remove necessary technical detail merely to make the article shorter.

## Paragraphs

Keep paragraphs reasonably short.

Most paragraphs should contain one main idea.

Use subheadings to make long articles easy to scan.

## Lists

Use lists where they improve clarity.

Do not turn the entire article into disconnected bullet points.

## Tables

Use tables for:

- Cost factors
- Option comparisons
- Timeline stages
- Responsibility breakdowns
- Material comparisons
- Decision criteria

Tables must remain readable on mobile devices.

## Claims

Do not claim:

- Guaranteed permit approval
- Guaranteed timelines
- Guaranteed return on investment
- Exact pricing without inspection
- Universal code requirements
- That HandKind is the best contractor
- That a project will be problem-free
- That every homeowner should renovate

# 13. Cannibalization Controls

Before creating a new article, determine whether it would compete with an existing page.

## Common Cannibalization Risks

- Multiple articles targeting the same city and service
- Separate cost articles with no meaningful difference
- Service pages and blog posts using the same commercial keyword
- Duplicate permit articles
- Multiple “complete guides” covering the same subject
- Annual updates published as separate pages instead of updating the existing article

## Preferred Solutions

When overlap exists:

1. Expand the stronger existing article.
2. Consolidate similar articles.
3. Redirect outdated pages.
4. Change the new topic to a different search intent.
5. Narrow the new article to a specific scenario.
6. Add a comparison or feasibility angle.
7. Update the existing article rather than creating a duplicate.

## Example

Do not publish:

- Kitchen Renovation Brantford
- Brantford Kitchen Renovations
- Best Kitchen Renovation Contractor Brantford
- Kitchen Remodeling Brantford

These all compete with the kitchen service page.

Instead publish:

- Kitchen Renovation Timeline in Brantford
- What Must Be Selected Before a Kitchen Renovation?
- Removing a Load-Bearing Wall During a Kitchen Renovation
- Temporary Kitchen Planning
- Stock vs Semi-Custom vs Custom Cabinets

# 24. Technical Publishing Workflow

Once an article has passed the Article Quality Checklist (Section 18) and has been drafted in the Required Submission Format (Section 19), use this workflow to get it live on handkindconstruction.ca. This is a mechanical process — it does not involve any further editorial judgment.

Total time: roughly 40 minutes per post. Publishing touches **four files**, not two — a new post is only fully wired in when all four are updated:

1. `blog/<slug>.html` — the post itself
2. `blog/posts.json` — post metadata
3. `blog/index.html` — the blog-index card **and** the CollectionPage schema
4. `sitemap.xml` — the URL entry

The site is a static site deployed from the `main` branch by GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/static.yml`) — there is no manual "upload to the host" step, and no server-side build that renders the index from `posts.json`. Everything below is committed to git and goes live when it lands on `main`.

## 24.1 Create the Post File (2 min)

Do **not** start from `blog/_TEMPLATE.html`. The template is missing the breadcrumb nav, canonical link, Open Graph / Twitter tags, and the dual `BlogPosting` + `BreadcrumbList` JSON-LD that every real post carries. Instead, copy the most recent published post (e.g. `blog/whole-home-renovation-cost-brantford.html`) and rename the copy with the slug from the article brief:

```
blog/your-post-slug-here.html
```

Slug rules:

- Lowercase, hyphenated, no special characters
- Matches the slug decided in Section 8 (Article Brief) / Section 19 (Recommended URL Slug)

Good slugs:

- `whole-home-renovation-cost-brantford.html`
- `home-addition-vs-aru-brantford.html`
- `renovate-or-move-brantford-paris.html`

## 24.2 Fill In the Post (20 min)

Work through the copied file top to bottom and replace every value carried over from the source post. In the `<head>`:

| Element | Update to |
|---|---|
| `<title>` | Article title + ` \| HandKind Construction` |
| `<meta name="description">` | Meta description from Section 19, under 160 characters |
| `<link rel="canonical">` | `https://handkindconstruction.ca/blog/your-slug.html` |
| `og:title`, `og:description`, `og:url`, `og:image` | Match the new title / description / URL; set the OG image (see 24.6) |
| `twitter:title`, `twitter:description`, `twitter:image` | Same as OG |
| `BlogPosting` JSON-LD | `headline`, `description`, `datePublished`, `dateModified`, `image`, `mainEntityOfPage` URL |
| `BreadcrumbList` JSON-LD + visible breadcrumb `<nav>` | Final crumb = this article's title |

In the body: set the `<h1>`, the post meta (category, location, date, read time ≈ 1 min per 200 words), then paste the finished article (Section 19, part 3) into the `post-body`, preserving the structure from Section 9:

- `<h2>` for main section headings
- `<p>` for paragraphs
- `<ul>` / `<li>` for lists of 3+ items
- `<blockquote>` for the key takeaway
- `<table>` for cost ranges or comparisons
- `<strong>` for important terms within paragraphs

Length: aim for 500–900 words for a standard supporting article. Pillar or cost/feasibility articles from the Priority Topic Roadmap (Section 16) may reasonably run longer — defer to the "Recommended Article Length" field from the brief rather than treating 900 words as a hard ceiling.

## 24.3 Update `posts.json` (3 min)

Add a new entry at the **top** of the array:

```json
{
  "slug": "your-post-slug-here",
  "title": "Your Post Title Here",
  "date": "2026-08-05",
  "author": "Adam McQuaig",
  "category": "Kitchens",
  "location": "Brantford",
  "excerpt": "One sentence excerpt under 160 characters — same as your meta description.",
  "image": "/assets/projects/your-hero.avif",
  "featured": true
},
```

If this post should be the featured one on the index (usually the newest), set its `"featured": true` **and flip the previously featured post to `"featured": false`.** Only one post is featured at a time.

## 24.4 Update `blog/index.html` (8 min)

This is the step the old workflow got wrong: the index does **not** rebuild itself from `posts.json`. The cards are hand-coded HTML, and the page's JavaScript only *filters* the existing DOM by category. Edit two separate places in `index.html`:

**(a) The card block** — between `<!-- BLOG_CARDS_START -->` and `<!-- BLOG_CARDS_END -->`, add the new post's `<a class="post-card">` as the first card. If it is the featured post:

- The new card gets `class="post-card post-card--featured"` and its `<img>` uses `loading="eager" fetchpriority="high"`.
- Demote the previous featured card to plain `class="post-card"` with `loading="lazy"`.
- Match the existing markup exactly (post-meta, `<h2 class="post-title">`, excerpt, read-more, author line with a formatted date). Write real descriptive `alt` text — not the bare title.

**(b) The CollectionPage schema** — in the `CollectionPage` JSON-LD near the top of the file, add the new URL as `"position": 1` in `mainEntity.itemListElement` and **renumber every following position**. (The separate `BreadcrumbList` block on the index does not change.)

> **Do not run `scripts/generate_blog_cards.py` to do this.** It regenerates the cards from `posts.json` but emits `alt="{title}"` and `loading="lazy"` for *every* card — silently wiping the hand-tuned alt text and the `eager`/`fetchpriority` on the featured image — and it does not touch the CollectionPage schema at all. Hand-edit the card to match the quality of the existing ones.

## 24.5 Update `sitemap.xml` (2 min)

Add a `<url>` entry at the top of the `<!-- Blog posts -->` block:

```xml
<url>
  <loc>https://handkindconstruction.ca/blog/your-post-slug-here.html</loc>
  <lastmod>2026-08-05</lastmod>
  <changefreq>monthly</changefreq>
  <priority>0.7</priority>
</url>
```

`<lastmod>` = the publish date.

## 24.6 Add the Hero Photo

Prefer a genuine HandKind project photo (Section 11). Most recent posts reuse an existing optimized image from `/assets/projects/` (`.avif`) or a social image from `/assets/og/`.

1. If adding a new file, export at ~1200px wide; use `.avif` / `.webp` to match the existing optimized assets, or `.jpg` for a social/OG image.
2. Reference it consistently in three places: the post's hero `<img>` (with real `alt` text), the `image` field in `posts.json`, and the card `<img>` in `index.html`.
3. Set the post's `og:image` to a 1200×630 social image in `/assets/og/`.

For a new card thumbnail, a 16:9 crop matches the index card layout.

## 24.7 Deploy (2 min)

Commit the four changed files (post HTML, `posts.json`, `index.html`, `sitemap.xml`) — plus any new image — on a branch and open a PR, matching the existing workflow (recent posts were merged via PRs to `main`). Merging to `main` triggers `.github/workflows/static.yml`, which deploys the whole repo to GitHub Pages. Google typically indexes the new URL within a day or two.

## 24.8 Adding a New Category

If a post needs a category beyond the current five (Kitchens, Bathrooms, Basements, Additions & ARUs, General), use it in `posts.json` **and** on the card's `data-cat` attribute in `index.html`. The filter buttons on the index auto-generate from the categories present in the DOM — no other template change needed.

## 24.9 Technical Publishing Checklist

Separate from — and in addition to — the editorial Article Quality Checklist (Section 18). Confirm before merging:

- [ ] Title includes "Brantford" or "Brant County" (or both), where locally relevant per Section 6.5
- [ ] Meta description under 160 characters and includes the primary keyword
