What we're proposing

An eight-sided timber pavilion, 26 feet by 22 feet, set on a stamped concrete floor. The front and sides stay open behind black wrought iron railings, so it feels part of the garden while giving shelter from sun and rain. Flush concrete walkways lead in through the east and west sides with no step at all, and the front steps down one riser to the grass.

The frame is heavy rough-sawn Douglas fir. Full eight-by-eight posts are each turned to face their corner, and stand on concealed steel bases so only a slim black plate shows at the floor. They carry beams tied together with curved knee braces, which form a soft arch between every pair of posts.

The back of the gazebo is a wall of light rather than a solid wall. Three arched stained-glass panels are held in open timber framing, with a knee wall below the centre panel clad in vertical cedar planks and topped with a cedar cap. Air moves freely around the glass and light comes through it. The glass in the model is a stand-in until the artwork is chosen.

Overhead, the steep roof is charcoal standing-seam steel, finished with a pyramid cap and a finial. Inside, the whole underside is lined with cedar tongue-and-groove boards stained to match the frame, so looking up is as good as looking out. A 60-inch ceiling fan hangs at the centre to keep air moving on warm days.

Colours on screen are approximate. Stain colours, the roof colour and the stained glass are chosen by St. Joseph's before we order.

Still to confirm

A few decisions are open. None of them hold up your review of the overall design.

  • The stained-glass artwork, panel sizes and how each panel is divided.
  • Stain colours for the timber and cedar, and the roof colour.
  • The curved braces, roof cap and overall height differ from the original permit drawings. The designer and engineer will review them before the drawings are updated.
  • How far the east and west walkways run, and where they join the existing garden paths.
